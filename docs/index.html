#30DayMapChallenge · Day 22 · Projections

Why do flights curve on the map?

Four real Lufthansa flights from Frankfurt (20–21 Nov 2026). Switch the projection: the routes stay the same, only the map changes.

Four flights

Why not fly straight?

A line that looks straight on a Mercator map keeps one compass heading all the way (a rhumb line). On a sphere that is not the shortest path. The shortest path is a great circle: stretch a string between two cities on a globe and it bows towards the pole.

Mercator stretches the map more and more towards the poles. So the great circle, which runs north, gets stretched into a curve, while the longer constant-heading line looks straight.

From FrankfurtShortest“Straight”Extra time

Extra time at 900 km/h. East–west routes gain the most; Cape Town, almost due south, gains nothing because both lines nearly coincide.

The shortest route to Los Angeles leaves Frankfurt heading north-west (318°), not west, and reaches 65° N over Greenland. The real LH456 did exactly that: 9,548 km, only 2% more than the shortest path.

Where real flights do detour

Three of the four real flights stayed within 2.2% of the shortest path. Tokyo is the exception: the shortest path crosses Russia, closed to European airlines since 2022. LH716 flew south of it, over the Black Sea, the Caucasus, the Caspian, Kazakhstan and China: 11,382 km, 1,998 km (21%) more — about 2 h 13 min longer at 900 km/h.

So the curve on the map is geometry. A real detour is politics or wind.

Four projections

Web Mercator keeps angles and shapes locally, which is why it is used for navigation and web maps. It does not keep distances: the further from the equator, the bigger everything gets.

Gnomonic shows every great circle as a straight line. The price: it can show less than half the Earth, and shapes blow up towards the edge. Old navigators drew the route on a gnomonic chart, then copied it onto Mercator to read the headings.

Globe (orthographic) is what you would see from space. Drag it to turn.

Distance from Frankfurt (azimuthal equidistant): every route from the centre is a straight line with its true length. The rings are 2,500 km apart.

Method

Real tracks: ADS-B positions from the adsb.lol open data archive (20–21 Nov 2026, ODbL), one Lufthansa flight per route (LH400, LH456, LH716, LH576). Track length is measured along the recorded positions; where no receiver heard the plane (oceans, parts of Asia) the gap is bridged by the shortest path and drawn dotted, so real tracks are, if anything, slightly longer. Airport coordinates for FRA, JFK, LAX, HND and CPT. Great circles are geodesics on the WGS84 ellipsoid (pyproj), drawn with d3-geo; rhumb-line lengths use a sphere of radius 6,371 km. Extra time = extra distance ÷ 900 km/h, a typical cruise speed without wind.

Limits

One flight per route on one day; other days differ. Real flights bend around jet streams (the North Atlantic Tracks are re-drawn twice a day), closed airspace (European airlines have avoided Russia since 2022, so Frankfurt–Tokyo detours), and overflight permits.
